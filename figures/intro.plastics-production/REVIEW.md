## Item 1. Plastics and life are in different units

The biomass values are carbon. The caption's plastics values are, at least in
part, total plastic mass. The two can't sit on one scale. There are two
consistent ways to fix it.

### Option A: everything in carbon (what the figures now use)

| Group | Gt C |
|---|---|
| Plants | 450 |
| Bacteria | 70 |
| Fungi | 12 |
| Archaea | 7 |
| Protists | 4 |
| Animals | 2 |
| Plastic, ever produced | 10.17 |
| Plastic, recycled | 1.40 |
| Plastic, incinerated | 2.05 |
| Plastic, remaining (landfill, environment, in use) | 6.71 |

Remaining plastic carbon is **3.4 times** the carbon in all animals.

Carbon fraction of plastic: 0.771 (notebook section 2.3; PE, PP, PVC, PET and
PS weighted by Geyer 2017 production shares; PUR and other polymers, 13.1% of
production, left out and the rest renormalized).

### Option B: everything in mass

Plastics as plastic mass, life as dry mass. Bar-On gives carbon only, so dry
mass here assumes carbon is about 50% of dry biomass. That is the usual
convention, and I believe Elhacham et al. 2020 (Nature 588, 442 to 444,
"Global human-made mass exceeds all living biomass") uses it, but check their
methods before relying on this.

| Group | Gt (dry mass for life) |
|---|---|
| Plants | 900 |
| Bacteria | 140 |
| Fungi | 24 |
| Archaea | 14 |
| Protists | 8 |
| Animals | 4 |
| Plastic, ever produced | 13.19 |
| Plastic, recycled | 1.82 |
| Plastic, incinerated | 2.66 |
| Plastic, remaining (landfill, environment, in use) | 8.70 |

Remaining plastic mass is **2.2 times** the dry mass of all animals.

### Recommendation

Option A. The biomass source is in carbon, the notebook already does the
conversion, and the comparison only needs one assumption (the plastic carbon
fraction) instead of two. Whichever we pick, cite Elhacham et al. 2020, which
makes the same comparison by mass.

## Item 2. Total production or what is still here

The life values are what exists now. "Plastic ever produced" (10.2 Gt C)
includes plastic that was incinerated, so its carbon is now in the atmosphere,
and plastic that was recycled into new products. The comparable number is the
plastic that still exists: 6.7 Gt C in landfill, the environment or in use.

Options:

1. Lead with the remaining amount and show total production as a dashed
   outline. Both demos do this.
2. Keep total production as the main circle with slices (current figures). This
   is honest about the breakdown, but the headline size is not comparable to
   the life groups.

Recommendation: option 1.

Two limits on "remaining" that the caption should state:

- It includes plastic still in use (the notebook says so in section 2.3).
- It does not separate managed landfill from plastic leaked into the
  environment. That split matters for an enzyme paper and the notebook can't
  provide it.

## Numerical problems for review

Each item has the current value, what the notebook says, and a proposed fix.

1. **"6.3 Gt recycled or incinerated" (caption).** The notebook gives 4.49 Gt of
   plastic (1.82 recycled + 2.66 incinerated), or 3.46 Gt C. My guess is that 6.3
   comes from Geyer 2017's headline of 6.3 Gt of plastic waste generated by 2015,
   which is a different quantity. Fix: use 4.5 Gt (mass) or 3.5 Gt C (carbon).

2. **"Of 13 Gt, 6.3 recycled or incinerated and 6.7 remains" (caption).** The
   13 is plastic mass, but the 6.7 is carbon: it is the notebook's remaining
   plastic in 2026 (8.70 Gt) times 0.771 = 6.71 Gt C. The sum only works
   because 6.3 was chosen to fill the gap. Fix: in carbon, 10.2 = 6.7 + 3.5;
   in mass, 13.2 = 8.7 + 4.5.

3. **Animal mass has three values.** 2.5 Gt in the figure, 2 Gt C in the
   notebook's 2026 table (Bar-On), 2.589 Gt in notebook section 2.3 (from Visual
   Capitalist, not a primary source). Fix: 2 Gt C from Bar-On. Done in the figures.

4. **"2.6-fold" (abstract line 56, caption line 75).** This is 6.71 / 2.589,
   which pairs plastic carbon with the Visual Capitalist animal value. With
   Bar-On's 2 Gt C it is 3.4-fold in carbon, or 2.2-fold by mass (option B).
   Fix: 3.4-fold if we go with option A.

5. **The notebook's 2026 table mixes units with no labels.** "Total 13.2,
   Recycled 1.8, Incinerated 2.6" are plastic mass. "Carbon Mass 10.2" is the
   carbon in all plastic produced, not in what remains (that is 6.7). Fix: add a
   units column and a "remaining" row to the table.

6. **Two reference years.** Section 2.3 uses 2024 (9.331 and 6.347 Gt C); the
   table and the figures use 2026. Fix: pick one year and state it in the
   caption. The figures and `plastics_numbers.py` use 2026 (`YEAR` at the top).

7. **Rate columns in the CSV are a tenth of the rate actually applied.** For
   2014 the "Recycling rate" column says 0.0175, but recycled plastic that year
   is 17.6% of the previous year's production, which matches Geyer's
   0.7 percentage points per year from 1990. The totals are right; the column
   values or headers are not. Fix: correct the columns so nobody reuses them.

8. **Model assumptions worth stating.**
   - All plastic becomes waste one year after production. Geyer 2017 uses
     product lifetimes by sector, from under a year for packaging to decades for
     construction. The one-year rule overstates waste in early years, and
     therefore overstates recycling and incineration.
   - Recycling and incineration rates are a straight line from one sentence in
     Geyer 2017, extended from 2014 to 2050.
   - Recycled plastic is subtracted for good and never re-enters waste.

   Fix: list these in the Methods or the figure legend. Using Geyer's own
   cumulative 2015 split as a check would also help (from memory: about 9%
   recycled, 12% incinerated, 79% in landfill or the environment; verify).

9. **Box sizes in the square figure.** Measured against the plants box: fungi is
   drawn at about 11.2 Gt C (label 12), archaea 6.7 (label 7), protists 3.9
   (label 4). The animals box is drawn at 2.0, which now matches its label. The
   old plastics square was drawn at about 5 (label 6.7); it has been replaced.
   Fix: resize fungi if the square version is kept.

10. **The data file lived only in Downloads.** It is now in `input/`, so the
    notebook runs from this folder.

11. **Biomass uncertainty.** Bar-On's estimates for bacteria and archaea carry
    roughly 10-fold uncertainty. Not an error, but one caption sentence would
    cover it.

## Design problems and the demos

Demos are in `demo/` (`make_demos.py` regenerates both from `plastics_numbers.py`).

| Problem | Demo 1 (log dot plot) | Demo 2 (focus circles) |
|---|---|---|
| The key comparison (plastic vs animals) is not shown directly | Bracket with "3.4× animals" between the two rows | Plastic placed next to animals, with an arrow labelled "3.4× the carbon in all animals" |
| Diameter axis reads as a linear scale | Real log axis with labelled ticks | No axis; areas and direct labels only |
| Plants takes most of the panel | Log scale puts all groups on one line | Plants and bacteria left out, with a note giving their size |
| Title does not match content | "Plastic Waste Holds More Carbon Than All Animals" | same |
| No year or sources | Subtitle with year, unit and sources | same |
| Low-contrast coloured labels | Labels in dark ink; colour only on the marks | same |
| Production vs what remains (item 2) | Filled dot for remaining, dashed ring for ever produced | Filled circle for remaining, dashed ring for ever produced |

Recommendation: Demo 1 for the paper, because it shows every group accurately
and the log axis can be read directly. Demo 2 is the stronger slide for a talk.

and neither is installed on this machine, so every render uses a fallback.
Pick one installed family for all of Figure 1.
