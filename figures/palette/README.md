# Plasticome fold palette

One colour per fold of the plasticome tree figure, with the hues taken from the
PETadex atlas so the figure and the site agree on what colour a fold is.

Generated by `tree/16_palette_reference.py`. The definition lives in
`tree/15_recolour_atlas.py`; this folder is output, not source.

## Where the hues come from

The atlas does not store a hex per fold. It stores a **base hue per CATH
superfamily**, in `petadex.io/frontend/src/utils/cathColors.js` as `CATH_HUE`,
and builds every colour it draws from that hue: `HSL(hue, 60, 38)` for the
superfamily and `HSL(hue, 70, 35 to 70)` for the components inside it. Tracking
the atlas therefore means **taking its hue assignment, not its hex values**. The
atlas hexes are tuned for light points on a dark map and would swamp the
substrate coloured tips if dropped in as slice backgrounds.

6 of the ten folds have a hue in the atlas. The other 4 CATH
superfamilies are not in the PETadex component set; the atlas would draw them in
its unknown grey, which is no use in a figure where they would then be
indistinguishable from each other and from the "Other folds" slice. They were
given hues in the atlas's own idiom, placed in the widest gaps left by the fixed
six (50 to 140, 140 to 210, 210 to 270, 270 to 360) so the ten stay as far apart
as the fixed assignments allow. They are marked `extended` below and should be
adopted into `CATH_HUE` on the site if the two are to stay in step.

## How each column is built

| Column | Construction | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| `tint` | `HSL(hue, 51.9, 89.0)` | the slice background. S and L are the means of the ten tints the figure used before, so the figure is no paler or stronger than it was and hue is the only thing that moved |
| `tint_second` | `shade(tint, 0.955)` | the alternating band that separates neighbouring components inside a slice |
| `label` | `HSL(hue, 40.0, 54.0)` | a fold label set in its own fold's colour. This is the canonical value; the labels in the current figure keep the individual saturation and lightness they were given by hand, with only their hue moved |
| `atlas_base` | `HSL(hue, 60, 38)` | the atlas's own superfamily colour, for anything that sits on a dark background |

The structure cartoons are drawn from `tint` directly, in 26 depth bands between
lightness factors 0.74 and 1.10, with outlines at 0.52 of each band.

## One thing to watch

The alpha/beta hydrolase fold takes the atlas's hue 210, which is blue, and its
tips are mostly PET blue. The palette it replaced used a warm tint there
deliberately, for contrast against those tips. The slice is still readable, but
it has less separation from its own tips than it had before. If that matters,
drop the hydrolase tint's saturation rather than moving its hue, so the figure
and the atlas still agree on the hue.
